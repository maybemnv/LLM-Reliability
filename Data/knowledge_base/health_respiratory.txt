Respiratory Diseases and Air Quality

Source: National Institute of Respiratory Health 2023

Introduction

The respiratory system is the primary interface between the human body and ambient air, making it particularly vulnerable to air pollution exposure. Respiratory diseases, including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), asthma, and lung cancer, are significantly influenced by air quality and represent major contributors to global morbidity and mortality.

Acute Respiratory Effects

Short-term exposure to elevated levels of air pollution can cause immediate respiratory symptoms:

Upper Respiratory Tract Effects
- Nasal and throat irritation
- Coughing and sneezing
- Increased mucus production
- Sore throat and hoarseness

Lower Respiratory Tract Effects
- Chest tightness and discomfort
- Shortness of breath
- Wheezing
- Exacerbation of existing conditions

Chronic Respiratory Conditions

Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)

COPD, encompassing chronic bronchitis and emphysema, is strongly associated with long-term air pollution exposure. Key findings include:

- PM2.5 exposure accelerates lung function decline
- Hospital admissions for COPD exacerbations increase during pollution episodes
- Air pollution exposure may increase COPD prevalence independent of smoking
- Rural populations using biomass fuels face elevated COPD risk

Asthma

Air pollution both triggers and potentially causes asthma:

- Ozone and PM2.5 provoke acute asthma attacks
- Children exposed to traffic-related pollution have higher asthma incidence
- Pollutants enhance allergic sensitization to common allergens
- Indoor air pollution from cooking fuels is a major global asthma contributor

Lung Cancer

The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) has classified outdoor air pollution and particulate matter as Group 1 carcinogens:

- PM2.5 exposure increases lung cancer risk by approximately 8% per 10 μg/m³
- Diesel exhaust is a well-established lung carcinogen
- Indoor radon exposure contributes significantly to lung cancer incidence
- Synergistic effects exist between air pollution and smoking

Respiratory Infections

Air pollution increases susceptibility to respiratory infections:

- Impaired mucociliary clearance reduces pathogen removal
- Weakened immune responses in the airways
- Increased bacterial adhesion to respiratory epithelium
- Higher rates of pneumonia and bronchitis during pollution episodes

Children and Respiratory Health

Children are particularly vulnerable to respiratory effects of air pollution:

1. Higher breathing rates relative to body size increase pollutant intake
2. Developing lungs are more susceptible to damage
3. Outdoor activity levels increase exposure
4. Early life exposure may have lasting effects on lung development
5. Air pollution exposure during pregnancy affects fetal lung development

Prevention and Management

Clinical Recommendations

Healthcare providers should:
- Assess patients' air pollution exposure
- Counsel high-risk patients on exposure reduction
- Adjust treatment plans during high pollution periods
- Recommend air quality monitoring for sensitive patients

Environmental Controls

Effective measures include:
- Indoor air filtration with HEPA filters
- Proper ventilation in homes and workplaces
- Avoidance of outdoor exercise during poor air quality days
- Source control for indoor pollutants

Conclusion

Respiratory health is inextricably linked to air quality. Reducing air pollution through policy interventions while implementing individual protective measures can significantly decrease the burden of respiratory disease.
